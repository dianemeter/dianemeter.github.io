UT BIOME · Nasal Modelling Subteam · Sep 2026 – present

OrthoBreathe

Helping Toronto startup Alinnovate design a device that helps people with allergies breathe easier. We're early in this project, and I'm really excited about it.

My part
Modelling and dimensioning the OrthoBreathe strap
Client
Alinnovate, a Toronto medical device startup
Team
UT Biomedical Engineering Design Team, Nasal Modelling subteam
Tools
SolidWorks, 2D drawings, ANSYS (learning)

Why I joined

I loved biology in high school. I find biological phenomena really interesting. Hot take from an engineering student (math, physics, chemistry), I know. Maybe I should've become a doctor, but I didn't want to spend all my time studying and reading textbooks. I enjoy hands-on work way more!

So finding ways to bring biology into engineering felt like the best of both worlds. It's also why I'm minoring in bioengineering, and UT BIOME is where I get to try it out on a real project. Some industries I'm looking into are medical devices and assistive technology.

The project

Alinnovate is developing OrthoBreathe, a medical device designed to relieve allergic rhinitis (basically, hay fever) by improving airflow through the nose.

Our subteam is building models that help answer the startup's research questions. That includes a reusable nasal cavity model with ports for humidity and temperature sensors, so the team can run airflow tests again and again.

Questions the client wants answered

  • How well does the nose hold humidity over time?
  • How do particles move through it?
  • How does oxygen uptake change?
  • Does cold air behave differently from warm air?

Strap modelling

I'm modelling the OrthoBreathe strap in SolidWorks, dimensioning it to match the current product so the client can check our model against the real thing.

Along the way, I'm learning a lot of new CAD and 2D drawing skills.

What's ahead

  1. NextModelling the nose itself. Organic shapes like the nasal cavity are a whole new challenge in CAD. The model will be used for airflow simulation in ANSYS, which I'm learning now.
  2. ThenChoosing a material. We'll compare 3D-printed and silicone versions of the reusable nasal model.
  3. End of termA research poster. We'll present everything Alinnovate asked us to explore, so the startup has a foundation to build on.

What I'm learning

I've worked with clients in my design courses, but this is my first time working with a startup. Our models aren't just exercises: they're the starting point Alinnovate will build its next steps on. That makes me want to get every dimension right.
