University of Toronto Wind Turbine Design Team · May 2026 – present

UTWind

I joined UTWind's mechanical subteam in the summer, right in the middle of the team's busiest stretch: assembling the turbine. Going in, I knew a wind turbine had three blades and a tower, and that was about it. A few months later, I'd helped build part of one, painted it, and started testing sustainable materials for 3D printing parts for the next one.

Summer 2026

Fall 2026 – present

Sustainable 3D printing

Getting algae, wood and biodegradable filaments to print, with tensile testing next.

What UTWind has taught me so far

I've learned that I learn best with my hands. Spending a summer touching every part of a turbine, from the drivetrain in the nacelle to the electrical boxes, gave me the big picture before I started on the details. Now that I'm doing more research and CAD, I know where each piece fits and why it matters.

It's also taught me to figure things out on my own. Nobody hands you print settings for algae filament; you test, look closely at what came out, and adjust. That mix of seeing the whole system and getting my hands on the parts is a big part of why I chose mechanical engineering.
